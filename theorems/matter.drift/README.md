#### the derivation

**\delta \cdot \hat{n} = c̄ - \frac{\lambda(\hat{n})}{pulse period}**  
<sub>premise · doppler/what-a-moving-source-writes-into-space</sub>  

read the other way round, the spacing between sign reversals at a place GIVES the component of the source's velocity along the line to it. That is a local reading - one place, one period, and no need to know which source it came from - and it is the velocity the source ACTUALLY has, because it is the displacement that shifted the fronts. So the emitter's velocity is already written into the phase pattern by rules that were there for other reasons, and a tag carrying a declared one adds no information. It is the declared number that is the weaker of the two: nothing in the dynamics sets it and nothing checks it against where the source goes

#### what the runs found

**`fcc-12`**

`doppler/what-a-moving-source-writes-into-space` - holds. G^XOR's source alternates its sign (+- over 4 ticks) and its rays leave one cell a tick, so the spacing between sign reversals along a direction is 2·(c̄ - δ·n̂) - compressed ahead, stretched behind. The emitter's velocity is therefore readable locally from the phase pattern, which is what the velocity label was standing in for and what makes it redundant

- the sign a source emits, tick by tick = 2 - over 4 ticks, the ticks on which the source had rays lit and their net sign: t0:+, t1:- - both signs appear, so it alternates. Read by ticking the world and looking at the rays, not by consulting the helper that decides it. Ticks where nothing was lit are left out rather than counted as a sign of nought
