# theorems

What the rules of `G` come to, derived from the rules themselves.

```
cd languages/.ts
npm run theorems
```

Nothing here is written by hand, and nothing here was measured. `Continuum` assembles the
continuous model by COUNTING what each rule's body does - the degree in the density is the
quantifier, the share is the gate's own expression, the sign is what the atoms do to the two
ledgers. `Prove` reads its premises off that same line and closes them under the inference
rules. This folder is what came out.

## which is a different guarantee from a measured one

The research repository's prover takes its leaves from PROBES: a fact with a run behind it,
refused unless something was measured. That is the right guarantee for what a world DID.

These leaves are read off the program. A premise here is not believed and not measured - it is
already in `G.ts`, and a rule edited there moves the conclusion. So a page can be checked by
reading the rule it names rather than by trusting a number.

## and there is no lattice on these pages

No box, no seed, no number of ticks, and no geometry. Those are settings a MEASUREMENT is about;
what is derived here is what the rules say, which they say on every lattice at once. `D` stays
a symbol for exactly that reason, and a two-dimensional lattice reads its own answer off the
same line.
