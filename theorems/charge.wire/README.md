#### the derivation

**q is conserved in flight**  
<sub>premise · carried/what-a-ray-keeps</sub>  

run with MOVEMENT and ARRIVAL and nothing else, the multiset of what the rays are carrying as polarity is exactly what it was 6 ticks earlier. Transport writes each ray's contents onto one neighbour and swaps them in, and polarity is part of what a ray carries - so it is moved and neither made nor unmade. Compared as a MULTISET rather than as a sum, so a rule that swapped two rays' labels would be caught rather than counted as conservative. This holds at every shell at once and for every run length, because it is a property of the rule. Set aside to ask it: EMISSION, CREATION, ANNIHILATION, TRANSPORT

**q goes every way alike**  
<sub>premise · carried/what-a-ray-keeps</sub>  

handed a source with no axis, G^XOR's own EMISSION rule wrote the same value of polarity on every one of its 6 exits - not most of them, all of them. So what a round source puts out goes every way alike, which is what says a share is the whole of it over the WHOLE shell rather than over some part of it

**shell about a wire = d(ball about a 1-dimensional source) / dr̄**  
<sub>premise · extent/room-around-a-source-of-any-size</sub>  

the sites at exactly r̄ steps from it are those within r̄ less those within r̄-1, which is the rate the room grows at - the same relation between a shell and a ball however much of space the source itself takes up

**n[q] about a wire ∝ \frac{q}{shell about a wire}**  
<sub>derived · spreading</sub>  

the same argument, over a different amount of room. what a ray carries as polarity is conserved on its way out and goes every way alike, so one site's share is the whole of it over however many sites there are at that distance - and how many that is depends on the shape of what it came from. Here that is shell about a 1-dimensional source rather than the shell about a point, and nothing else in the argument changes

**ball about a 1-dimensional source(r̄) = |r̄·P_\perp ∩ L|**  
<sub>premise · extent/room-around-a-source-of-any-size</sub>  

```
ball about a 1-dimensional source(r̄) = 41, 123, 205, 287, 369, 451, 533
Δ^(1) = 82, 82, 82, 82, 82, 82
```

the sites within r̄ steps of a source spanning 1 direction are the r̄-fold dilate of the sites within one step of it, in the 1 directions ACROSS it - the source already spans the other 1, so nothing grows that way. Checked: the counts walked out of the lattice are a polynomial in r̄ of degree 1, which is D-1, exactly and on integers

**ball about a 1-dimensional source(r̄) ∝ STEP·r̄^(D-1)**  
<sub>derived · Ehrhart's theorem</sub>  

```
ball about a 1-dimensional source(r̄) = |r̄·P ∩ L|
= β·r̄^(D-1) + c_1·r̄^(D-1-1) + ... + c_D-1
∝ β·r̄^(D-1)
```

Ehrhart 1962: the number of lattice points in the k-fold dilate of a lattice polytope is a polynomial in k of degree exactly D, whose leading coefficient is the polytope's volume. Here k is r̄ and P is the set of sites one step from the centre, so the ball's count is a polynomial in r̄ of degree D-1 and everything below the leading term is dropped by the proportionality

**shell about a wire(r̄) ∝ STEP·r̄^(D-2)**  
<sub>derived · the difference of a dilate</sub>  

```
shell about a wire(r̄) = ball about a 1-dimensional source(r̄) - ball about a 1-dimensional source(r̄-1)
= β·r̄^(D) - β·(r̄-1)^(D)
= β·r̄^(D) - β·(r̄^(D) - D·r̄^(D-1) + ...)
= β·D·r̄^(D-1) + ...
∝ β·r̄^(D-1)
```

every site is either within r̄-1 steps or it is not, so the shell is the ball less its own shift by one step. Expanding (r̄-1)^(D) by the binomial theorem, the leading β·r̄^(D) cancels against the ball's, and what survives is D·β·r̄^(D-1). The D came DOWN from the exponent in that cancellation; a proportionality does not carry it, and the exponent left behind is D-1

**n[q] about a wire ∝ \frac{q}{STEP·r̄^(D-2)}**  
<sub>derived · expansion</sub>  

shell about a 1-dimensional source is not a primitive of this theory - it is what the line above shows it to be, so it stands in for itself here
