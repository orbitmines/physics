#### the derivation

**what the waiting makes = \sigma·\rho**  
<sub>rule · MOVEMENT</sub>  

```
no rays, 1 of space
the waiting makes \sigma·\rho
```

MOVEMENT hands the ray to itself and grows the world - no ray made, destroyed or moved, and a point of space where there was none. That is a carrier standing still to make the room it could not step into, and the rate it does so at is \sigma·\rho

**a_0 = \sigma·\rho**  
<sub>theorem · the room the line does not supply, which the waiting has to make</sub>  

```
the space line carries a term with no rays in it: the waiting
a ray that cannot step makes the room instead, and that is space at \sigma·\rho
a_0 = \sigma·\rho
```

the space ledger gains one wherever a free point splits and loses one wherever two carriers meet, so its net rate per point is the first less the second. NOTHING IS FITTED HERE: it is the space line read off as it stands, and it is the only scale in this theory that is not a count of the tiling

**c̄ = 1**  
<sub>rule · MOVEMENT</sub>  

every active ray goes ONE CELL along its own exit in one tick, which is what the streaming term says - so a step is one cell, and that is the only length the lattice has to measure anything else against

**v = \frac{n}{\paren{n + \sigma·\rho}}**  
<sub>theorem · how fast a carrier goes, when the cell ahead may not be there yet</sub>  

```
MOVEMENT: either(some(to), step, waitForRoom(it))
waitForRoom carries space: count(1) and no step
room to be made: a_0 = \sigma·\rho a point a tick, shared by n carriers
each waits a_0/n of a tick and steps with the rest
v = n/(n + a_0) = \frac{n}{\paren{n + \sigma·\rho}}
```

MOVEMENT says a lit ray goes one cell along its exit, and then says what happens when there is no cell: waitForRoom, which MAKES a point of space and does not step. That is a carrier being refused a cell, and it is in these rules. A ray therefore advances only on the ticks the cell ahead was already there, and what has to be made is what the space line makes - a_0 a point a tick. CREATION makes it where a point is free; where carriers are standing, they make it, and n of them share it, so each spends a_0/n of its tick standing still. Where the medium is dense none of them ever waits and the speed is exactly the one cell a tick MOVEMENT advertises. Where it is thin the speed goes as the density itself
