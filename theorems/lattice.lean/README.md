# lattice.lean

> what is one annihilation worth to a path, on this lattice?

Proved under `G`, `G^XOR`, `G^XOR*2`. Generated by `implementations/.ts/src/theorems` - nothing here was written by hand: every premise is a count or an exact invariance taken from the theory itself, and the conclusion is what the inference rules made of them. ## G

### the result

**lean = \frac{c̄}{DEG} = 1/12**

| lattice | D | DEG | box | ticks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fcc-12` | 3 | 12 | 21 | 20 |

#### the derivation

**lean = \frac{c̄}{DEG}**  
<sub>definition · lattice.lean</sub>  

an annihilation gives the direction it went one extra way of being taken, while every other way out of that point still weighs exactly what it did before - and there are DEG of those. So the lean is a step against DEG: what the annihilation bought, over the alternatives it did not take. The numerator is c̄ rather than a bare 1 because what it bought is a step, and a step is c̄

**c̄ = 1**  
<sub>premise · counts/what-the-tiling-fixes</sub>  

a step's worth of lean is what one annihilation buys a path, and a step is c̄ - one cell a tick, the lattice's own speed. Its value in those units is one, which is a statement about the units and not about the quantity. A count of the tiling, taken off the geometry itself with nothing run and nothing fitted

**DEG = 12**  
<sub>premise · counts/what-the-tiling-fixes</sub>  

the ways out of a point on fcc-12 - the exits it has, counted. A count of the tiling, taken off the geometry itself with nothing run and nothing fitted

**lean = \frac{c̄}{DEG} = 1/12**  
<sub>derived · a ratio of counts</sub>  

```
lean = \frac{c̄}{DEG}
= \frac{1}{12}
= 1/12
```

c̄ is 1 and DEG is 12, both counted off the tiling, so the ratio is 1/12 exactly - and it is worth reading as c̄/DEG rather than as the number, because on another lattice it is a different number and the same ratio

#### what the runs found

`counts/what-the-tiling-fixes` - holds. fcc-12: D = 3, DEG = 12, SHEET = 6, CYCLE = 6 - counts, not measurements

- D = 3 - how many dimensions fcc-12 has
- DEG = 12 - the ways out of a point on fcc-12 - the exits it has, counted
- SHEET = 6 - the exits with no component along the pulsing axis, which is what a source emits over. NOT DEG, which is 12
- CYCLE = 6 - how many steps go round the ring of a turn on fcc-12
- slice = 2 - a turn sweeps a PLANE, and a plane needs two dimensions - so what a turn lives in is min(D, 2), which is 2 here and stops growing at 2 however many dimensions are added
- c̄ = 1 - a step's worth of lean is what one annihilation buys a path, and a step is c̄ - one cell a tick, the lattice's own speed. Its value in those units is one, which is a statement about the units and not about the quantity
