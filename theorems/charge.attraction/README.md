#### the derivation

**F = F_vac + F_q**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

and what a body actually feels is everything that arrives, which is BOTH channels - and only one of them has been touched. The vacuum channel needs neither body to emit anything: a body's cells are not neutral, the split does not fire on them, and the expansion that did not happen spreads outward as a shortfall whatever signs anybody is carrying. THERE IS NOTHING IN IT FOR A BIAS TO ACT ON, so it is the same for both signs of charge and cannot be screened by either. That is the part of this law that is gravity, and it is why a charged thing falls like an uncharged one even where the other channel is pulling it about

**F_q = F_meet · g_q**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

and a pair that meets opposed that fraction as often feels that fraction of the meeting channel. This line says nothing except what a ratio is

**g_q = \frac{opposed}{share}**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

gravity.full writes the meeting channel as six factors multiplied - what one meeting is worth to a path, how much of the time the two are OPPOSED, the charges each pulse lets go on both sides, the two masses, and the meetings summed along the line. Exactly one of those asks about the SIGNS the bodies are carrying, and it is the second: the rest are counts of the tiling, masses, and a distance, and not one of them moves when a body is biased. So the biased and unbiased channels differ by that factor alone and everything else cancels out of the ratio

**share = \frac{n_opp}{n_states}**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

two charges are opposed exactly when their meeting leaves nothing - that is what the word means for a rule that either annihilates a pair or does not. The states a pair can be in are equally available, so how much of the time they are opposed is how many of those states annihilate, over how many there are

<sub>premise · bias/what-two-biased-bodies-do</sub>  

ANNIHILATION leaves nothing exactly when the pair is opposite - enumerated over a real facing pair, - with -: both still there; - with +: nothing left; + with -: nothing left; + with +: both still there - so how often two bodies meet opposed is how often one offers a + while the other offers a -, either way round. At biases P_a and P_b that is (1+P_a)(1-P_b)/4 + (1-P_a)(1+P_b)/4, which multiplies out to (1 - P_a·P_b)/2 - exactly, over all 49 bias pairs, to 0.000000. And the 1254 meetings actually run at the corners and the middle agree with it to 0.0440 against a sampling error of 0.0316. NOTE WHAT IS NOT ASSUMED: not that the rule destroys the opposite pairs, which was enumerated; and not that the two draws are independent, which is what drawing them from two bodies makes true

**F = F_vac + F_q**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

and what a body actually feels is everything that arrives, which is BOTH channels - and only one of them has been touched. The vacuum channel needs neither body to emit anything: a body's cells are not neutral, the split does not fire on them, and the expansion that did not happen spreads outward as a shortfall whatever signs anybody is carrying. THERE IS NOTHING IN IT FOR A BIAS TO ACT ON, so it is the same for both signs of charge and cannot be screened by either. That is the part of this law that is gravity, and it is why a charged thing falls like an uncharged one even where the other channel is pulling it about

**F_q = F_meet · g_q**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

and a pair that meets opposed that fraction as often feels that fraction of the meeting channel. This line says nothing except what a ratio is

**F = F_meet·g_q + F_vac**  
<sub>derived · substituting</sub>

F = F_meet·g_q + F_vac
```

F_q is not a primitive of this theory - it is F_meet·g_q, so it stands in for itself here and the result is multiplied out

**g_q = \frac{opposed}{share}**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

gravity.full writes the meeting channel as six factors multiplied - what one meeting is worth to a path, how much of the time the two are OPPOSED, the charges each pulse lets go on both sides, the two masses, and the meetings summed along the line. Exactly one of those asks about the SIGNS the bodies are carrying, and it is the second: the rest are counts of the tiling, masses, and a distance, and not one of them moves when a body is biased. So the biased and unbiased channels differ by that factor alone and everything else cancels out of the ratio

**opposed = \frac{1 - P_a·P_b}{2}**  
<sub>premise · bias/what-two-biased-bodies-do</sub>  

ANNIHILATION leaves nothing exactly when the pair is opposite - enumerated over a real facing pair, - with -: both still there; - with +: nothing left; + with -: nothing left; + with +: both still there - so how often two bodies meet opposed is how often one offers a + while the other offers a -, either way round. At biases P_a and P_b that is (1+P_a)(1-P_b)/4 + (1-P_a)(1+P_b)/4, which multiplies out to (1 - P_a·P_b)/2 - exactly, over all 49 bias pairs, to 0.000000. And the 1254 meetings actually run at the corners and the middle agree with it to 0.0440 against a sampling error of 0.0316. NOTE WHAT IS NOT ASSUMED: not that the rule destroys the opposite pairs, which was enumerated; and not that the two draws are independent, which is what drawing them from two bodies makes true

**share = \frac{n_opp}{n_states}**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

two charges are opposed exactly when their meeting leaves nothing - that is what the word means for a rule that either annihilates a pair or does not. The states a pair can be in are equally available, so how much of the time they are opposed is how many of those states annihilate, over how many there are

**share = \frac{n_opp}{n_states}**  
<sub>derived · as a ratio</sub>  

share is annihilating over cases, which written out is \frac{annihilating}{cases} - kept in the counts rather than worked out, so that whatever uses it can cancel against them

**g_q = 1 - P_a·P_b**  
<sub>derived · one over another</sub>  

```
g_q = opposed / share
= (1/2 - 1/2·P_a·P_b) / (\frac{n_opp}{n_states})
= 1 - P_a·P_b
```

opposed is 1/2 - 1/2·P_a·P_b and share is \frac{annihilating}{cases}, so the one over the other is 1 - P_a·P_b

**F = F_meet - F_meet·P_a·P_b + F_vac**

#### the derivation

**F = F_vac + F_q**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

and what a body actually feels is everything that arrives, which is BOTH channels - and only one of them has been touched. The vacuum channel needs neither body to emit anything: a body's cells are not neutral, the split does not fire on them, and the expansion that did not happen spreads outward as a shortfall whatever signs anybody is carrying. THERE IS NOTHING IN IT FOR A BIAS TO ACT ON, so it is the same for both signs of charge and cannot be screened by either. That is the part of this law that is gravity, and it is why a charged thing falls like an uncharged one even where the other channel is pulling it about

**F_q = F_meet · g_q**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

and a pair that meets opposed that fraction as often feels that fraction of the meeting channel. This line says nothing except what a ratio is

**F = F_meet·g_q + F_vac**  
<sub>derived · substituting</sub>  

```
F = F_q + F_vac
F_q = F_meet·g_q
F = F_meet·g_q + F_vac
```

F_q is not a primitive of this theory - it is F_meet·g_q, so it stands in for itself here and the result is multiplied out

**g_q = \frac{opposed}{share}**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

gravity.full writes the meeting channel as six factors multiplied - what one meeting is worth to a path, how much of the time the two are OPPOSED, the charges each pulse lets go on both sides, the two masses, and the meetings summed along the line. Exactly one of those asks about the SIGNS the bodies are carrying, and it is the second: the rest are counts of the tiling, masses, and a distance, and not one of them moves when a body is biased. So the biased and unbiased channels differ by that factor alone and everything else cancels out of the ratio

**opposed = \frac{1 - P_a·P_b}{2}**  
<sub>premise · bias/what-two-biased-bodies-do</sub>  

ANNIHILATION leaves nothing exactly when the pair is opposite - enumerated over a real facing pair, - with -: both still there; - with +: nothing left; + with -: nothing left; + with +: both still there - so how often two bodies meet opposed is how often one offers a + while the other offers a -, either way round. At biases P_a and P_b that is (1+P_a)(1-P_b)/4 + (1-P_a)(1+P_b)/4, which multiplies out to (1 - P_a·P_b)/2 - exactly, over all 49 bias pairs, to 0.000000. And the 1254 meetings actually run at the corners and the middle agree with it to 0.0440 against a sampling error of 0.0316. NOTE WHAT IS NOT ASSUMED: not that the rule destroys the opposite pairs, which was enumerated; and not that the two draws are independent, which is what drawing them from two bodies makes true

**share = \frac{n_opp}{n_states}**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

two charges are opposed exactly when their meeting leaves nothing - that is what the word means for a rule that either annihilates a pair or does not. The states a pair can be in are equally available, so how much of the time they are opposed is how many of those states annihilate, over how many there are

**share = \frac{n_opp}{n_states}**  
<sub>derived · as a ratio</sub>  

share is annihilating over cases, which written out is \frac{annihilating}{cases} - kept in the counts rather than worked out, so that whatever uses it can cancel against them

**g_q = 1 - P_a·P_b**  
<sub>derived · one over another</sub>  

```
g_q = opposed / share
= (1/2 - 1/2·P_a·P_b) / (\frac{n_opp}{n_states})
= 1 - P_a·P_b
```

opposed is 1/2 - 1/2·P_a·P_b and share is \frac{annihilating}{cases}, so the one over the other is 1 - P_a·P_b

**F = F_meet - F_meet·P_a·P_b + F_vac**  
<sub>derived · substituting</sub>  

```
F = F_meet·g_q + F_vac
g_q = 1 - P_a·P_b
F = F_meet - F_meet·P_a·P_b + F_vac
```

g_q is not a primitive of this theory - it is 1 - P_a·P_b, so it stands in for itself here and the result is multiplied out

**F = F_vac + F_q**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

and what a body actually feels is everything that arrives, which is BOTH channels - and only one of them has been touched. The vacuum channel needs neither body to emit anything: a body's cells are not neutral, the split does not fire on them, and the expansion that did not happen spreads outward as a shortfall whatever signs anybody is carrying. **F_q = F_meet · g_q**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

and a pair that meets opposed that fraction as often feels that fraction of the meeting channel. This line says nothing except what a ratio is

**F = F_meet·g_q + F_vac**  
<sub>derived · substituting</sub>  

```

F = F_meet·g_q + F_vac
```

**g_q = \frac{opposed}{share}**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

gravity.full writes the meeting channel as six factors multiplied - what one meeting is worth to a path, how much of the time the two are OPPOSED, the charges each pulse lets go on both sides, the two masses, and the meetings summed along the line. Exactly one of those asks about the SIGNS the bodies are carrying, and it is the second: the rest are counts of the tiling, masses, and a distance, and not one of them moves when a body is biased. So the biased and unbiased channels differ by that factor alone and everything else cancels out of the ratio

**opposed = \frac{1 - P_a·P_b}{2}**  
<sub>premise · bias/what-two-biased-bodies-do</sub>  

ANNIHILATION leaves nothing exactly when the pair is opposite - enumerated over a real facing pair, - with -: both still there; - with +: nothing left; + with -: nothing left; + with +: both still there - so how often two bodies meet opposed is how often one offers a + while the other offers a -, either way round. At biases P_a and P_b that is (1+P_a)(1-P_b)/4 + (1-P_a)(1+P_b)/4, which multiplies out to (1 - P_a·P_b)/2 - exactly, over all 49 bias pairs, to 0.000000. And the 1254 meetings actually run at the corners and the middle agree with it to 0.0440 against a sampling error of 0.0316. NOTE WHAT IS NOT ASSUMED: not that the rule destroys the opposite pairs, which was enumerated; and not that the two draws are independent, which is what drawing them from two bodies makes true

**share = \frac{n_opp}{n_states}**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

two charges are opposed exactly when their meeting leaves nothing - that is what the word means for a rule that either annihilates a pair or does not. The states a pair can be in are equally available, so how much of the time they are opposed is how many of those states annihilate, over how many there are

**share = \frac{n_opp}{n_states}**  
<sub>derived · as a ratio</sub>  

share is annihilating over cases, which written out is \frac{annihilating}{cases} - kept in the counts rather than worked out, so that whatever uses it can cancel against them

**g_q = 1 - P_a·P_b**  
<sub>derived · one over another</sub>  

```
g_q = opposed / share
= (1/2 - 1/2·P_a·P_b) / (\frac{n_opp}{n_states})
= 1 - P_a·P_b
```

opposed is 1/2 - 1/2·P_a·P_b and share is \frac{annihilating}{cases}, so the one over the other is 1 - P_a·P_b

**F = F_meet - F_meet·P_a·P_b + F_vac**

```

g_q = 1 - P_a·P_b
F = F_meet - F_meet·P_a·P_b + F_vac

That is the part of this law that is gravity, and it is why a charged thing falls like an uncharged one even where the other channel is pulling it about

**F_q = F_meet · g_q**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

and a pair that meets opposed that fraction as often feels that fraction of the meeting channel. This line says nothing except what a ratio is

**F = F_meet·g_q + F_vac**  
<sub>derived · substituting</sub>  

```
F = F_q + F_vac
F_q = F_meet·g_q
F = F_meet·g_q + F_vac
```

F_q is not a primitive of this theory - it is F_meet·g_q, so it stands in for itself here and the result is multiplied out

**g_q = \frac{opposed}{share}**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

gravity.full writes the meeting channel as six factors multiplied - what one meeting is worth to a path, how much of the time the two are OPPOSED, the charges each pulse lets go on both sides, the two masses, and the meetings summed along the line. Exactly one of those asks about the SIGNS the bodies are carrying, and it is the second: the rest are counts of the tiling, masses, and a distance, and not one of them moves when a body is biased. So the biased and unbiased channels differ by that factor alone and everything else cancels out of the ratio

**opposed = \frac{1 - P_a·P_b}{2}**  
<sub>premise · bias/what-two-biased-bodies-do</sub>  

ANNIHILATION leaves nothing exactly when the pair is opposite - enumerated over a real facing pair, - with -: both still there; - with +: nothing left; + with -: nothing left; + with +: both still there - so how often two bodies meet opposed is how often one offers a + while the other offers a -, either way round. At biases P_a and P_b that is (1+P_a)(1-P_b)/4 + (1-P_a)(1+P_b)/4, which multiplies out to (1 - P_a·P_b)/2 - exactly, over all 49 bias pairs, to 0.000000. And the 1254 meetings actually run at the corners and the middle agree with it to 0.0440 against a sampling error of 0.0316. NOTE WHAT IS NOT ASSUMED: not that the rule destroys the opposite pairs, which was enumerated; and not that the two draws are independent, which is what drawing them from two bodies makes true

**share = \frac{n_opp}{n_states}**  
<sub>definition · charge.attraction</sub>  

two charges are opposed exactly when their meeting leaves nothing - that is what the word means for a rule that either annihilates a pair or does not. The states a pair can be in are equally available, so how much of the time they are opposed is how many of those states annihilate, over how many there are

**share = \frac{n_opp}{n_states}**  
<sub>derived · as a ratio</sub>  

share is annihilating over cases, which written out is \frac{annihilating}{cases} - kept in the counts rather than worked out, so that whatever uses it can cancel against them

**g_q = 1 - P_a·P_b**  
<sub>derived · one over another</sub>  

```
g_q = opposed / share
= (1/2 - 1/2·P_a·P_b) / (\frac{n_opp}{n_states})
= 1 - P_a·P_b
```

opposed is 1/2 - 1/2·P_a·P_b and share is \frac{annihilating}{cases}, so the one over the other is 1 - P_a·P_b

**F = F_meet - F_meet·P_a·P_b + F_vac**  
<sub>derived · substituting</sub>  

```
F = F_meet·g_q + F_vac
g_q = 1 - P_a·P_b
F = F_meet - F_meet·P_a·P_b + F_vac
```

g_q is not a primitive of this theory - it is 1 - P_a·P_b, so it stands in for itself here and the result is multiplied out
