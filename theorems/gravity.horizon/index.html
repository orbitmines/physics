gravity.horizon

the shortfall grows without limit as a body is approached, and a point has only so many exits to be missing. What happens where the two meet?

G

\[\text{S at the horizon} = \bar{DEG} \paren{r^{\paren{D - 1}}}\]

the derivation

\(\delta \text{ is conserved on its way out}\) theorem · every rule is a function of its own match
every one of the 7 terms the line carries is a rule acting on the match it was handed
none of them consults anything outside it, so agreement is carried and disagreement is only moved
the DENSITY is pushed back at a = 2 \sigma F \rho + -\paren{\nu \paren{0 - \bar{DEG} \paren{\rho^{\paren{\bar{DEG} - 1}}}}}, which is a different quantity

a rule reads the refs its match handed over and nothing else, so two worlds that agree about a match agree after it - a difference between them can be moved and cannot be made or unmade. What a meeting takes from the disturbed world it leaves as a hole in the undisturbed one, and the difference is the same size. So as much of the shortfall crosses a far shell as a near one, which is what the dilution argument wants

\(\delta \text{ goes every way alike}\) theorem · the lattice
exits(p): DEG ways out, none of them special

a point's exits are alike and a rule reads none of them by name, so what leaves a place goes every way alike

\(shell \propto r^{\paren{D - 1}}\) theorem · how many places are r steps out
exits(p): the ways out of a point, DEG of them
steps: where one exit leads - and that is all the rules say about where anything is
D of those ways are independent, so places within r steps ∝ r^{D}
shell(r) = ball(r) - ball(r-1) ∝ r^{D-1}

the rules give a point, DEG ways out of it, and where each one leads - and nothing else about where anything is. So how far is how many steps were taken, and how many places are that far out is a count of walks. D is how many of those ways are independent, so within r steps there are r choices along each of D and the places within r go as r^{D}; the places at exactly r are the difference between two of those, which is r^{D-1}. Every step is whole and every place counted once, so this is the count itself and not an approximation to one

\(v = \frac{1}{\paren{1 + n_{f}}}\) theorem · the turn
the choice is 1 way straight on against n_{f} folded
v = \frac{1}{\paren{1 + n_{f}}}

the turn draws one way straight on against the folded ways of the place, so the share of a step that carried a ray on is one against one plus the record

\(\delta \text{ per site} = \frac{\paren{\delta \paren{1 + n_{f}}}}{\paren{r^{\paren{D - 1}}}}\) theorem · spreading
what crosses one site: δ/shell = \frac{\delta}{\paren{r^{\paren{D - 1}}}}
and it dwells 1/v there, with v = \frac{1}{\paren{1 + n_{f}}}
δ per site = \frac{\paren{\delta \paren{1 + n_{f}}}}{\paren{r^{\paren{D - 1}}}}

count what crosses a shell in one tick - the sites on it, times what is at each, times the share of a step that went outward - and MOVEMENT neither makes nor destroys, so that count is carried outward unchanged. So what CROSSES one site is the whole of it over the number of sites there are at that distance. What IS at one site is that again over how fast a share gets across

\(\text{the ways out of a point} = \bar{DEG}\) rule · CREATION
the body lit DEG exits
so a point has DEG ways out

CREATION lights every exit a point has, so the count its body ran over is how many ways out there are - DEG. A shortfall is ways out that are missing, so that count is also its ceiling

\(\text{S at the horizon} = \bar{DEG} \paren{r^{\paren{D - 1}}}\) theorem · where the falloff meets the ceiling
\delta per site = \frac{\paren{\delta \paren{1 + n_{f}}}}{\paren{r^{\paren{D - 1}}}}
\delta per site \le \bar{DEG}, the ways out a point has
S = \bar{DEG} \paren{r^{\paren{D - 1}}}
so on three dimensions a mass goes as the AREA of its horizon, not as its radius

the shortfall per site goes as S/r^{D-1} and cannot exceed DEG, the ways out a point has. Setting the two equal and solving for the SOURCE rather than for the radius gives the strength needed to put a horizon at r as the ceiling times the room out there
