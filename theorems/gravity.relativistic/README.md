**F_g^(rel) = F_g · retardation · γ^{m_r-m_s}**  
<sub>definition · gravity.relativistic</sub>

**γ = (1-β^(2))^(-1/2)**  
<sub>premise · budget/what-a-tick-is-spent-on</sub>  

the clock advances by the crossing component, which is the root of that - so a moving thing ticks at sqrt(1 - β^(2)) and one lattice tick is worth (1 - β^(2))^(-1/2) of its own. The Lorentz factor, out of a step of fixed length and nothing else

**γ = (1-β^(2))^(-1/2)**

#### the derivation

**F_g^(rel) = F_g · retardation · γ^{m_r-m_s}**  
<sub>definition · gravity.relativistic</sub>  

so the force is the one between bodies at rest, times what retardation does to what arrives, times gamma to whatever power the clocks call for. k is +1 if the answer is counted in the receiver's own ticks and -1 if the source is the one moving - a numerator reduction, since a slowed source pulses less often - and 0 in the lattice's own frame, which is where the dynamics run

**γ = (1-β^(2))^(-1/2)**  
<sub>premise · budget/what-a-tick-is-spent-on</sub>  

the clock advances by the crossing component, which is the root of that - so a moving thing ticks at sqrt(1 - β^(2)) and one lattice tick is worth (1 - β^(2))^(-1/2) of its own. The Lorentz factor, out of a step of fixed length and nothing else

**γ = (1-β^(2))^(-1/2)**  
<sub>derived · kept exact</sub>  

γ is (1-β^(2)) to the -1/2, and it is carried as that rather than expanded. The exponent is not a whole positive number, so a series would be infinite and would have to be cut somewhere - and anything built on the cut version inherits the cut. Kept closed it is exact

**F_g^(rel) = \frac{F_g·retardation}{(1-β^(2))^{(m_r-m_s)/2}}**  
<sub>derived · substituting</sub>  

```
F_g^(rel) = F_g·retardation·γ^{m_r-m_s}
γ = \frac{1}{(1-β^(2))^(1/2)}
F_g^(rel) = \frac{F_g·retardation}{(1-β^(2))^{(m_r-m_s)/2}}
```

γ is not a primitive of this theory - it is \frac{1}{(1-β^(2))^(1/2)}, so it stands in for itself here and the result is multiplied out

**retardation = w·ahead + (1-w)·behind**  
<sub>definition · gravity.relativistic</sub>  

how much of the time you are on each side is w and 1 - w, so what arrives is the two branches at those weights. what arrives from the compressed branch goes as one over that - carried as an exact power rather than expanded, so nothing below inherits a truncation

**ahead = (1-β)^(-1)**  
<sub>derived · kept exact</sub>

behind is not a primitive of this theory - it is \frac{1}{(1+β)}, so it stands in for itself here and the result is multiplied out

**retardation = \frac{num(retardation)}{den(retardation)}**  
<sub>derived · over a common denominator</sub>
