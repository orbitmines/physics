# charge.beyond

> a charge's field falls off as one over the square of the distance. How much of it is left beyond a radius r?

#### the derivation

**ε = \int_r^(∞) 1/s^(2) ds**  
<sub>definition · charge.beyond</sub>  

everything past r is everything at every s from r outwards, added up - and there is no outer edge to stop at, so the upper limit is however far you care to go

**1/s^(2) = \frac{1}{s^(2)}**  
<sub>definition · charge.beyond</sub>  

what a charge leaves at a distance falls off as one over the square of it - which is gravity.falloff at D = 3, cited rather than counted again

**ε = \frac{1}{r}**  
<sub>derived · integrating a power</sub>  

```
ε = \int_r^(Infinity) \frac{1}{s^(2)} ds
= \frac{s^(-1)}{-1}, at the limits
= \frac{1}{r}
```

what is at s is \frac{1}{s^(2)}, a power -2 of s. Integrated that is s^(-1)/-1, taken between r and Infinity
