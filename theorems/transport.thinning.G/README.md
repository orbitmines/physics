# transport.thinning - G

> a medium carries something outward from a source. How thick is it at r̄ steps, and does that change when the carriers slow down in thin medium?

### the derivation

**Phi is conserved in flight**  
<sub>definition · transport.thinning</sub>

**Phi = shell · n · v**  
<sub>definition · transport.thinning</sub>  

what crosses a shell in a tick is the number of sites on it, times how much medium is at each, times how fast that medium is moving through. That is what a flux IS, and it mentions no distance

**shell(r̄) = ball(r̄) - ball(r̄-1)**  
<sub>premise · lattice/what-the-tiling-is</sub>  

a site is either within r̄-1 steps or it is not, so the sites at exactly r̄ steps are those within r̄ less those within r̄-1 - a subtraction, with nothing left over and nothing counted twice

**ball(r̄) = |r̄·P ∩ L|**  
<sub>premise · lattice/what-the-tiling-is</sub>  

```
ball(r̄) = 1, 13, 55, 147, 309, 561, 923, 1415, 2057
Δ^(1) = 12, 42, 92, 162, 252, 362, 492, 642
Δ^(2) = 30, 50, 70, 90, 110, 130, 150
Δ^(3) = 20, 20, 20, 20, 20, 20
```

the sites within r̄ steps are the r̄-fold dilate of the sites within one step - take one step r̄ times and you are somewhere in r̄·P, and every site of r̄·P is reached that way. Checked: the counts walked out of the lattice are a polynomial in r̄ of degree 3, which is D, exactly and on integers

**ball(r̄) ∝ r̄^(D)·β**  
<sub>derived · Ehrhart's theorem</sub>  

```
ball(r̄) = |r̄·P ∩ L|
= β·r̄^(D) + c_1·r̄^(D-1) + ... + c_D
∝ β·r̄^(D)
```

Ehrhart 1962: the number of lattice points in the k-fold dilate of a lattice polytope is a polynomial in k of degree exactly D, whose leading coefficient is the polytope's volume. Here k is r̄ and P is the set of sites one step from the centre, so the ball's count is a polynomial in r̄ of degree D and everything below the leading term is dropped by the proportionality

**shell(r̄) ∝ r̄^(D-1)·β**  
<sub>derived · the difference of a dilate</sub>  

```
shell(r̄) = ball(r̄) - ball(r̄-1)
= β·r̄^(D) - β·(r̄-1)^(D)
= β·r̄^(D) - β·(r̄^(D) - D·r̄^(D-1) + ...)
= β·D·r̄^(D-1) + ...
∝ β·r̄^(D-1)
```

every site is either within r̄-1 steps or it is not, so the shell is the ball less its own shift by one step. Expanding (r̄-1)^(D) by the binomial theorem, the leading β·r̄^(D) cancels against the ball's, and what survives is D·β·r̄^(D-1). The D came DOWN from the exponent in that cancellation; a proportionality does not carry it, and the exponent left behind is D-1

**v ∝ c**  
<sub>definition · transport.thinning</sub>  

a carrier moves at whichever bound is smaller, and in the dense regime that is the lattice's: there are partners everywhere, so the hand-off is never what a carrier waits for and the lattice's one-cell-a-tick is the binding bound. Both bounds were counted off the theory rather than assumed - what is chosen here is only which of them a carrier is waiting on, which is a fact about how thick the medium is at a place

**n ∝ 1 / (r̄^(D-1)·c·β)**  
<sub>derived · balancing a conserved product</sub>  

```
Phi = shell · n · v = constant
r̄^(D-1)·c·n·β = constant
n^(1) ∝ 1 / (r̄^(D-1)·c·β)
n ∝ 1 / (r̄^(D-1)·c·β)
```

Phi cannot change, and written out it is r̄^(D-1)·c·n·β. n carries the exponent 1 there, so holding the product fixed pins n to the rest raised to -1

## what it leans on

- **Ehrhart 1962** - Sur les polyèdres rationnels homothétiques à n dimensions, Eugène Ehrhart (1962). the number of lattice points in the k-fold dilate of a lattice polytope P is a polynomial in k of degree equal to the dimension of P, whose leading coefficient is the volume of P <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehrhart_polynomial>
- **the binomial theorem** - the binomial theorem. (x - 1)^n = x^n - n·x^(n-1) + ... , so subtracting a degree-n polynomial from its own shift by one cancels the leading term and leaves degree n-1
