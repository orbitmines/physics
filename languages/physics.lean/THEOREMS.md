## What is checked, and what is not

A step of the prover is a READING (a rule's own body, the continuous model, the lattice, a count: a premise, by design) or an INFERENCE (Inferences.ray, and the methods of Prove.ray that build the equation's chain). An inference records the line it concluded, not what it did to the lines it stands on - so that is read off its own code: it is run again on the store as it stood when the step arrived, with the lines it stands on standing as their NAMES, and what it concludes then is the relation it applies (`λ = ρ / what is taken` where the step says `λ = 1/(σFρ)`). The relation is a premise, named after the inference; the step is CHECKED when Lean proves that the relation, with those lines put in, is the step's line. Also checked: a step that names again what an earlier one named, and the answer (the last step with the settled numbers - a rate is one per match per tick, the facing factor a half - put in). Gen samples both sides first and offers only what agrees; Lean then has to prove it symbolically. `Defined` says where the lines are: what the prover divides by is not nought, and what it raises to a real power is positive - its algebra cancels across them.

What stays NOT CHECKED, each with its reason in the module: an inference that concludes nothing when its lines stand as names (it looks inside a value, or a guard goes the other way); a relation holding a root in the density that the lines it stands on are written in (the prover puts them in under the root by name; a prover's substitution does not); a relation that, with the lines put back, is not the line (the inference did something else to them than it does to names); a cancellation of a sum carrying a real power (1 - q^R), which the algebra of a field cannot show is not nought; and the equation chain's readings - the line solved along a heading, the past taken as infinite, the sum over bodies - which are not algebra at all.
